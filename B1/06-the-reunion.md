# The Reunion

Rachel almost didn't go. The invitation, which had appeared in her inbox
weeks earlier, was for a university reunion — ten years since she and her
closest friends had graduated. She had lost touch with most of them, and the
idea of seeing everyone again made her unexpectedly anxious.

"What if nobody remembers me the way I remember them?" she asked her partner
the night before.

"What if that doesn't matter?" he replied. "Just go and see."

The reunion was held in a hotel that had clearly been chosen for its size
rather than its charm. Rachel stood near the entrance for a moment, scanning
the room, before she spotted a familiar face: Daniel, who had barely changed
except for a beard he hadn't had before.

"Rachel!" He crossed the room immediately. "I wasn't sure you'd come."

"Neither was I," she admitted, laughing.

They talked for almost an hour before Sophie found them, someone Rachel had
been particularly close to during their final year, and whom she hadn't
spoken to in at least five years.

"I've thought about you so many times," Sophie said, hugging her. "I always
meant to message, and then somehow years passed."

It struck Rachel how easily the conversation returned to how it used to be,
as if no time had passed at all, even though so much had changed for all of
them. Daniel now had two children. Sophie had moved twice and changed careers
completely. Rachel herself had been through a divorce that none of them knew
about yet.

As the evening went on, old memories surfaced — trips they had taken, an
apartment they had shared that had terrible heating, a professor whose class
none of them had ever fully understood. There was something comforting about
being known by people who had seen an earlier version of her.

Near the end of the night, Sophie suggested they exchange numbers again and
actually use them this time.

"We say that every time," Daniel pointed out, smiling.

"Maybe this time we mean it," Rachel said.

Walking to her car afterward, she realized she felt lighter than she had in
weeks. Whatever had made her anxious about coming seemed distant now, replaced
by something closer to gratitude.

## New Words

- **reunion** — an event where people who have not seen each other for a
  while meet again
- **graduate** — to finish a course of study successfully
- **charm** — the quality of being pleasant or attractive
- **scan** — to look quickly over something or somewhere
- **strike** (somebody) — to make somebody suddenly notice or think of
  something
- **surface** — to appear or come into view
- **comforting** — making you feel less worried or sad
- **distant** — far away, or feeling far away in memory
- **gratitude** — a feeling of being thankful

# A Health Scare

Owen had been putting off the appointment for months. The chest pains had
started gradually, and he had convinced himself they were caused by stress at
work, or too much coffee, or simply getting older. It was his wife, Grace, who
finally insisted he see a doctor.

"You've been rubbing your chest for weeks," she said. "I've noticed, even if
you think I haven't."

The appointment itself was quick. The doctor listened to his heart, asked a
series of questions, and then, in a tone that was calm but serious, said he
wanted to run some tests immediately.

"It's probably nothing," the doctor added, "but it's better to be certain."

Those two hours, waiting for the test results, were some of the strangest of
Owen's life. He sat in the waiting room, scrolling through his phone without
really reading anything, imagining conversations he might have to have with
his children.

When the doctor finally called him back in, his expression gave nothing away.

"Your heart is fine," he said. "The pain was most likely caused by a muscle
strain, possibly from how you're sitting at your desk. I'd recommend seeing a
physiotherapist."

Owen felt something in his chest loosen that had nothing to do with muscles.

"That's it? A strain?"

"That's it," the doctor confirmed, smiling slightly. "Though I'd still
suggest you get your stress levels checked while you're at it."

Driving home, Owen felt oddly emotional, as if he had been given a second
chance at something, even though nothing had actually been wrong. He called
Grace from the car.

"It was nothing serious," he told her. "A muscle strain."

"Thank god," she said, and he could hear real relief in her voice.

That evening, they sat outside longer than usual, not talking about anything
important, just being together. Owen found himself noticing small things he
usually rushed past — the sound of the wind, how tired Grace looked after her
own long day, how rarely they simply sat like this.

"It's easy to take for granted the people who are always there," he admitted
eventually.

"Most people do," Grace said. "Until something reminds them not to."

## New Words

- **gradually** — slowly, over a period of time
- **strain** — an injury caused by using a muscle too much
- **oddly** — in a strange or unusual way
- **take for granted** — to fail to appreciate something because it is always
  present

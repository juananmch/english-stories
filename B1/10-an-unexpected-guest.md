# An Unexpected Guest

Grace hadn't spoken to her brother in almost three years when he called to
ask if he could stay for a few days. The last time they had spoken, it had
ended badly — an old disagreement about their parents' house that neither of
them had ever properly resolved.

"I wouldn't ask if I had another option," he said on the phone, his voice
tired in a way she didn't recognize. "Things haven't worked out here, and I
need somewhere to stay while I figure out what's next."

Grace hesitated. If she said yes, she would be opening a door she had spent
years keeping firmly closed. If she said no, she suspected she would regret
it, whatever the outcome.

"You can stay," she said finally. "But I'm not pretending everything's fine."

"I wouldn't expect you to."

He arrived two days later, thinner than she remembered and carrying only one
bag. Her husband, Marcus, who had heard the whole complicated history, greeted
him politely but watched the situation carefully over the following days.

The first evening was tense. They talked about safe subjects — his job that
hadn't worked out, the weather, nothing that came near the actual argument
that had separated them. Grace found herself waiting for old irritation to
return, and was almost surprised when it didn't, not in the way she had
expected.

On the third night, after their daughter had gone to bed, her brother finally
brought it up.

"I've thought about that argument more than you probably realize," he said.
"I said things I didn't mean. I was angry about things that had nothing to
do with you, and I took it out on you anyway."

Grace hadn't expected an apology, and certainly not one so direct.

"I said some things too," she admitted. "I could have called, over the
years. I told myself it was up to you, but that wasn't really fair."

Neither of them resolved everything that night — three years of silence
doesn't disappear over one conversation — but something shifted. By the time
he left a week later, with a temporary job and a small apartment lined up, it
no longer felt like an ending.

"Call me when you're settled," Grace told him at the door.

"I will," he said. "I promise, this time."

She believed him.

## New Words

- **disagreement** — a situation where people do not agree
- **resolve** — to find a solution to a problem or argument
- **hesitate** — to pause before doing or deciding something because you are
  unsure
- **outcome** — the result of an action or situation
- **tense** — feeling worried or nervous, unable to relax
- **irritation** — a feeling of slight anger
- **settled** — comfortable and established in a new place or situation

# Lost in Translation

Rachel and Owen had been planning their trip to Portugal for months, and they
were determined to see more than the usual tourist spots. On their third day,
they decided to take a bus to a small mountain village that a friend had
recommended.

The problem started at the bus station. Neither of them spoke much
Portuguese, and the woman at the ticket counter, who was clearly in a hurry,
spoke very little English. Owen pointed at the name of the village on his
phone and held up two fingers.

"Two tickets," he said slowly, as if speaking louder would help her
understand.

The woman nodded and printed two tickets. It wasn't until they were sitting on
the bus, an hour into the journey, that Rachel noticed something was wrong.

"This doesn't look right," she said, checking the map on her phone. "We're
going the wrong direction completely."

They got off at the next stop, a town neither of them had heard of, feeling
frustrated and slightly panicked. An older man who was waiting at the bus
stop noticed them studying their phones with confused expressions.

"Are you lost?" he asked, in careful English.

Rachel explained where they had been trying to go, showing him the name on her
phone. The man laughed, not unkindly.

"Ah, I understand the problem," he said. "There are two villages with almost
the same name. One is in the mountains, which is probably what you wanted. The
other, which is where your ticket was for, is near the coast."

He walked them to the correct platform and even waited until their bus
arrived, explaining the situation to the driver so there would be no more
confusion.

By the time they reached the mountain village, it was late afternoon, and they
had lost most of the day. Instead of feeling annoyed, though, they found
themselves laughing about it over dinner.

"We should have written the name down more clearly," Rachel admitted.

"Or learned ten words of Portuguese before we came," Owen added.

Despite the delay, the village turned out to be exactly what they had hoped
for: quiet streets, a small church, and a restaurant where the owner insisted
on choosing their meal for them. Looking back, Rachel decided that getting
lost had been, in its own strange way, one of the best parts of the trip.

## New Words

- **determined** — having decided firmly to do something
- **counter** — a long table where you are served in a shop or office
- **nod** — to move your head up and down to show agreement
- **frustrated** — annoyed because something is not going the way you want
- **panicked** — feeling sudden strong fear or worry
- **confusion** — a state of not understanding what is happening
- **annoyed** — slightly angry

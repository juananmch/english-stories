# Starting a Business

Grace had baked bread as a hobby for years before anyone suggested she should
sell it. It was her neighbor, Owen, who first said it out loud, after eating
an entire loaf in one sitting.

"You could genuinely sell this," he told her. "I'm not exaggerating."

Grace laughed it off at first. She had a full-time job in accounting, and the
idea of running a business on top of that seemed impossible. But the comment
stayed in her mind, and over the following weeks, she found herself thinking
about it more seriously.

She started small: a few loaves sold to friends and coworkers, ordered
through a shared spreadsheet. Word spread faster than she expected. Within
two months, she was baking every evening and most of the weekend, and she was
still turning down orders because she couldn't keep up.

"This is a good problem to have," Owen told her, "but it's still a problem."

Grace knew he was right. If she wanted to grow this into something real, she
would need proper equipment, a registered kitchen, and, eventually, her own
space. That meant taking a financial risk that made her nervous every time she
thought about it seriously.

She spent a month researching: business loans, health regulations, the cost
of a commercial oven. The numbers were intimidating, but not impossible. Her
savings, combined with a small loan, would be enough to rent a tiny unit on
the edge of town.

The hardest part was telling her manager she was resigning. She had worked at
the accounting firm for eight years, and leaving felt like stepping off solid
ground.

"Are you sure about this?" her manager asked.

"No," Grace admitted. "But I think that's normal."

Six months later, her small bakery was open three days a week. It wasn't
glamorous — she was up at four every morning, and the early months had been
exhausting and stressful in ways she hadn't fully anticipated. Still, when she
watched customers walk out with warm bread under their arms, she felt certain
she had made the right choice.

"Told you," Owen said, when he came in for his usual loaf.

"You did," Grace agreed. "I should probably thank you properly one day."

## New Words

- **hobby** — an activity you do for enjoyment, not for money
- **exaggerate** — to make something sound bigger or more extreme than it is
- **spreadsheet** — a document used for organizing numbers and lists
- **registered** — officially recorded on a list
- **loan** — money that a bank lends you, to be paid back later
- **regulation** — an official rule
- **intimidating** — making you feel nervous or frightened
- **commercial** — used for business rather than personal use
- **resign** — to formally leave a job
- **glamorous** — attractive and exciting in an impressive way
- **anticipate** — to expect something before it happens

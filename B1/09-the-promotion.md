# The Promotion

Daniel had wanted the team leader position for over a year, ever since his
manager, who had since retired, first mentioned that he had the right skills
for it. When the role was finally advertised, he applied without hesitation.

The interview process was longer than he had expected: two rounds, a
presentation, and a panel of people he had never met before. By the time he
was offered the position, he felt more relieved than excited.

His colleague Rachel, who had also applied but hadn't been selected, was the
first person he told.

"I got it," he said, unsure how she would react.

"Daniel, that's brilliant," she said, and seemed to mean it. "You deserve it.
Genuinely."

Still, the first weeks were more complicated than he had anticipated. He was
now managing people who, until recently, had been his equals. Meetings that
used to be casual conversations became something else entirely. He found
himself second-guessing decisions that, in his old role, he would have made
without a second thought.

The hardest moment came when he had to give feedback to a team member whose
work had been consistently below standard. It was a conversation he had
avoided having as a colleague, precisely because it hadn't been his
responsibility. Now, avoiding it wasn't an option.

He prepared for the conversation more than he had prepared for almost
anything at work. When it happened, it went better than he had feared — the
team member, who admitted she had been struggling with personal issues, was
grateful someone had finally addressed it directly, rather than letting it be
ignored.

"I thought you'd be angry," she told him afterward.

"I just want us to figure it out together," he said, surprising himself with
how naturally the words came.

By the third month, the discomfort of being someone's boss instead of their
equal had faded into something more like confidence. He still asked Rachel
for advice occasionally, and she gave it honestly, without any resentment
about the position she hadn't gotten.

"You're better at this than you think," she told him one afternoon.

"I'm mostly just making it up as I go," Daniel admitted.

"That," Rachel said, "is basically what leadership is."

## New Words

- **hesitation** — a pause caused by feeling unsure
- **panel** — a group of people chosen to make a decision or judge something
- **brilliant** — excellent
- **deserve** — to have earned something through your actions or qualities
- **casual** — relaxed and informal
- **second-guess** — to doubt or question a decision after making it
- **feedback** — comments about how well or badly someone has done something
- **consistently** — in the same way, again and again
- **struggle** — to have a lot of difficulty doing something
- **discomfort** — a feeling of being uncomfortable or uneasy
- **resentment** — a feeling of anger about something unfair
- **leadership** — the ability or act of leading a group of people

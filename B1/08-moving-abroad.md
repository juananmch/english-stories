# Moving Abroad

Sophie had lived in three different countries by the time she turned
thirty-five, but nothing had prepared her for how strange the first few
months in Japan would feel.

She had moved there for Marcus's job, a decision they had made together after
long conversations about what they both wanted from the next chapter of their
lives. In theory, she had been excited. In practice, the early weeks were
harder than she had expected.

Everyday tasks that used to be automatic — buying groceries, understanding a
bill, making small talk with a neighbor — suddenly required effort. She
couldn't read most signs. She misunderstood simple instructions constantly.
Once, she accidentally bought a bag of what she thought was sugar and
discovered, too late, that it was salt.

"I feel like a child again," she told Marcus one evening, only half joking.
"I can't do anything without help."

"You've been here six weeks," he reminded her. "Give it time."

She knew he was right, but knowing it didn't make the frustration disappear.
The isolation was the hardest part. Back home, she had a wide circle of
friends and a job she was confident in. Here, she had neither, and the
language barrier made it difficult to build either quickly.

Slowly, though, things began to shift. She started taking a language class
twice a week, which gave her a small group of people in a similar situation —
other foreigners, equally lost, equally determined. She found a café near
their apartment where the owner, patient with her limited vocabulary, began
greeting her by name.

By the sixth month, she noticed something had changed without her fully
realizing it. She read a sign without translating it in her head first. She
had a short conversation with a delivery driver that went smoothly. She
recognized a shortcut through the neighborhood that saved ten minutes.

"I think I finally feel like I live here," she told Marcus, "rather than just
visiting."

He smiled. "That's usually how it works. It just takes longer than anyone
warns you."

Looking back later, Sophie would say that those difficult early months had
taught her more about herself than any of the comfortable years before them.

## New Words

- **automatic** — done without thinking, from habit
- **misunderstand** — to understand something incorrectly
- **isolation** — the state of being alone or separated from others
- **barrier** — something that makes it difficult to do something
- **foreigner** — a person from another country
- **shortcut** — a quicker way of doing or reaching something

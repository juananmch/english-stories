# A Family Argument

Daniel and his father used to talk on the phone every Sunday, without fail.
That routine had stopped three months ago, after an argument neither of them
had quite recovered from.

It had started over something small. Daniel's father had made a comment about
how often Daniel and his wife, Sophie, visited — or rather, how rarely.

"You used to come every month," his father had said. "Now we barely see you."

Daniel, tired after a difficult week at work, hadn't handled it well.

"We have jobs, Dad. We have a life here. It's not always easy to just drop
everything and drive four hours."

The conversation had gone downhill from there. Things were said that both of
them regretted, though neither had apologized. Daniel told himself his father
was being unreasonable. His father, presumably, told himself the same thing
about Daniel.

Sophie had watched the silence grow over the following weeks. She noticed
that Daniel avoided mentioning his father, and that a birthday card was still
sitting on the shelf, never sent.

"You should call him," she said one evening.

"He should call me," Daniel replied, though he didn't sound entirely
convinced.

"Does it matter who calls first?"

Daniel didn't have a good answer to that. He thought about his father, who
had raised him mostly alone after his mother passed away, working long hours
and never once, as far as Daniel could remember, missing a school event.

The following Sunday, out of habit more than decision, Daniel picked up the
phone.

His father answered on the second ring, as he always had.

"I've been thinking," Daniel said, before either of them could bring up the
argument, "that we should visit next weekend. All three of us."

There was a pause on the line.

"I'd like that," his father said finally. "I'm sorry about what I said."

"Me too," Daniel replied.

Neither of them mentioned the argument again after that call, but the
following Sunday, they were sitting at his father's kitchen table, and
somehow that felt like enough. Sophie watched them from across the room,
noticing how easily they fell back into their old jokes, as if three months of
silence had simply been erased. She didn't say anything about it. Some things
were better left alone, and this, she thought, was clearly one of them.

## New Words

- **unreasonable** — not fair or sensible
- **presumably** — probably, as far as one can tell
- **convinced** — certain that something is true
- **pause** — a short stop in speaking or action
- **erase** — to remove something completely, as if it had never happened

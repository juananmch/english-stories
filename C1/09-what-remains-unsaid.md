# What Remains Unsaid

Simon's mother had been dead for six months before he mentioned her, other
than in the most functional terms, to anyone outside his immediate family —
a fact that struck several of his closer friends as characteristically
consistent with a man who had never, so far as any of them could recall,
discussed anything genuinely difficult with anything approaching openness.
That he grieved was not in question; that he intended to do so in silence
seemed, to those who knew him, simply the manner in which he had always
conducted his interior life.

It was Priya, his colleague of some years and increasingly a friend beyond
the confines of the office, who eventually declined to accept this
arrangement. Not through any dramatic confrontation — she was, if anything,
constitutionally averse to those — but through a persistent, low-key
refusal to let his deflections pass entirely unremarked.

"You don't have to tell me anything," she said one evening, having noticed,
not for the first time, how deftly he'd redirected a conversation that had
drifted, however briefly, toward the subject of his mother. "I would just
rather you knew that I've noticed you doing that, in case it ever stops
feeling like the only option available to you."

He said nothing in response, that evening, beyond a noncommittal
acknowledgment that could have meant almost anything. It was not until
several weeks later, on an evening distinguished by nothing in particular,
that he found himself, somewhat to his own bewilderment, telling her about
the last conversation he'd had with his mother — an ordinary, unremarkable
exchange about nothing consequential, made unbearable only in retrospect by
the knowledge that it had been the last.

"I keep thinking I should have said something else," he admitted, with a
rawness that felt, even as he spoke, almost unfamiliar coming from his own
mouth. "Something that mattered. Instead we talked about whether I'd
remembered to get the car serviced."

"Would it have changed anything," Priya asked, gently, "if you had said
something else?"

He considered this longer than the question perhaps warranted. "No," he
said, eventually. "I don't suppose it would have. She'd have known regardless.
I think that's rather the point I keep missing — that the last conversation
doesn't have to have been profound for the relationship behind it to have
been."

Priya said nothing further, recognizing, correctly, that the observation
required no response beyond having been heard. What had shifted, in the
weeks that followed, was not that Simon became a notably more forthcoming
person in general — he did not, by most measures, change very much at all —
but that he had, at least once, permitted someone else to witness the
grief he had spent six months curating so carefully out of view, and had
found, somewhat to his surprise, that the world had not become
correspondingly more difficult to inhabit as a result.

He mentioned his mother again, unprompted, some months later, in a manner so
brief and offhand that Priya nearly missed its significance entirely — a
passing remark about a recipe of hers he'd finally attempted himself,
successfully, after several failed prior efforts. It was not a
confession, this time, nor did it seem to cost him anything particular to
say. Priya recognized it, correctly, as considerably more meaningful for
precisely that reason.

## New Words

- **functional** (of speech) — limited to practical necessity, without
  emotional depth
- **constitutionally** (figurative) — by fundamental nature or character
- **averse** — having a strong dislike or reluctance toward something
- **deflection** — an act of avoiding or redirecting a difficult subject
- **deftly** — skillfully, in a smooth and practiced way
- **noncommittal** — not expressing a clear opinion or feeling
- **bewilderment** — a state of confusion, often about one's own reaction
- **rawness** — an unguarded, exposed emotional quality
- **curate** (figurative) — to carefully select and control what is shown

## Questions

3. What did Simon eventually tell Priya about?
4. What did Simon say he kept thinking he should have done differently?
5. What conclusion did Simon reach about that last conversation?

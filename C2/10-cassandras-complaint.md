# Cassandra's Complaint

Rosalind had spent the better part of eleven years warning, with steadily
diminishing confidence that anyone in a position of consequence was
actually listening, about a systemic vulnerability in the region's water
infrastructure that she now watched, with an emotion considerably more
complicated than vindication, unfold on the evening news exactly as she had
long predicted it would.

The reports she had authored across that span, each more urgent and more
thoroughly substantiated than the last, had received, in sequence, polite
acknowledgment, gentle deferral, a brief flurry of interest following a
smaller, containable incident that had briefly seemed to validate her
concerns before being quietly reclassified as an isolated anomaly, and
finally, in recent years, something closer to weary indifference from
officials who had, she suspected, come to regard her as a competent but
tiresomely persistent Cassandra, her expertise acknowledged in the abstract
while her actual recommendations remained, budget cycle after budget
cycle, comprehensively unfunded.

Being right, she was discovering in real time, provided considerably less
satisfaction than she might once have naively assumed it would. There was
no triumphant vindication available to her, only the queasy recognition
that the very outcome she had spent a decade trying to prevent had now
occurred in precisely the manner and for precisely the reasons she had
specified, down to details that felt, watching the crisis unfold, less like
prophecy fulfilled than like a particularly cruel form of professional
déjà vu.

Her former colleague Theo, calling that evening ostensibly to check on her
but transparently also curious how she was processing the moment,
attempted a note of grim congratulation she found she could not quite
receive in the spirit intended. "You were right," he said. "Publicly,
unambiguously right. That has to count for something."

"It counts for nothing that matters," she replied, with more bitterness than
she had intended to display. "Being right eleven years too early and unheeded
is functionally indistinguishable from being wrong. The people affected
tonight don't care that I filed the correct report in year one. They care
that the infrastructure failed regardless, and no amount of retroactive
credit changes what happened to them or what it will cost to actually
repair it now, under emergency conditions, at several multiples of what
prevention would have required."

Theo, to his credit, did not attempt to argue her out of the bitterness,
recognizing it as neither irrational nor, in the circumstances, remotely
excessive. What he offered instead, after a considered pause, was a
narrower and more useful observation: that her frustration, however
justified, risked obscuring the one genuinely actionable lesson available
to her now — that the failure had been institutional rather than
personal, a systematic incapacity to act on unwelcome expertise until
crisis rendered inaction impossible, and that her energies, once the
immediate emergency response concluded, might be better directed toward
that structural question than toward the admittedly tempting but largely
unproductive satisfaction of having been, all along, entirely correct.

She sat with the observation longer than she expected to, turning it over
until something in it settled. By morning, she had already begun drafting a
different kind of document than the ones that had occupied the previous
decade — not another warning about the specific failure now unfolding, but
a proposal for the sort of institutional mechanism that might, eventually,
let the next correct and unwelcome report actually be heard the first time,
rather than the eleventh.

## New Words

- **deferral** — the act of postponing or putting off a decision
- **flurry** — a brief, sudden burst of activity or interest
- **queasy** (figurative) — uneasy or uncomfortable, often about a moral
  situation
- **prophecy** — a prediction, especially one that later proves accurate
- **unheeded** — ignored despite being expressed or warned
- **retroactive** — applying to or affecting things that happened in the
  past
- **actionable** — able to be acted upon; providing a clear basis for action

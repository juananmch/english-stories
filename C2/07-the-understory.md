# The Understory

Desmond had returned, after an absence of eleven years, to the forest tract
his grandfather had logged nearly to bare earth in the final, indifferent
decade of an industry that had since largely moved elsewhere, expecting to
find something close to the desolation he remembered from his last visit:
stumps weathering slowly into the soil, a canopy so thoroughly removed that
sunlight fell, unfiltered and somehow accusatory, onto ground that had not
felt it so directly in over a century.

What he found instead confounded the tidy narrative of irreversible
devastation he had, without quite realizing it, been carrying for over a
decade. A dense, chaotic understory had asserted itself across the clearing,
opportunistic species crowding in first — the ecological equivalent, his
companion Rosalind observed, of first responders rather than permanent
residents — followed, in patches, by the slower, more tentative emergence
of species that suggested, cautiously, the beginnings of something more
closely resembling the forest that had once stood there, though it would be,
by any honest accounting, decades yet before the resemblance became
convincing.

"It's not the same forest," Rosalind said, watching him take in the
transformation with an expression she recognized as somewhere between relief
and a more complicated species of grief. "It won't be, not in either of our
lifetimes, maybe not in your grandchildren's either. But it isn't the
wasteland you were bracing for, and I think it's worth sitting with the
distinction rather than collapsing it into either 'ruined' or 'recovered,'
because neither word does justice to what's actually happening here."

He found himself turning the observation over for the remainder of the
walk, recognizing in it, with a discomfort he did not immediately examine
too closely, an uncomfortable parallel to his own life in the years since
his divorce — a period he had privately catalogued, with the same crude
binary Rosalind had just dismantled, as simple, irreversible ruin, a thing
broken beyond meaningful repair rather than a system, however damaged,
still quietly engaged in the slow, unglamorous, and entirely undramatic work
of reconstituting itself into something that would never replicate what had
existed before and might, nonetheless, eventually prove genuinely
sufficient on its own terms.

He did not articulate this parallel aloud, suspecting it would sound
mawkish stated so directly, and uncertain, in any case, that he had fully
worked out what it implied about his own circumstances beyond the vague,
provisional comfort of the analogy itself. What he permitted himself,
walking back toward the car as the light began to fail, was the more modest
observation that recovery, in forests as in less measurable domains of
experience, rarely announces itself with anything so legible as a single,
identifiable moment of completion — that it proceeds, instead, by an
accumulation of small, unremarkable persistences, each easy to overlook
individually and unmistakable only in retrospect, once enough of them have
quietly accumulated to constitute something a person might, with appropriate
caution, begin calling a forest again.

Rosalind, watching him fall quiet for the remainder of the drive, did not
press him to articulate whatever he was working through, recognizing,
correctly, that some conclusions arrive more honestly when given room to
finish forming on their own schedule rather than under the pressure of
being explained aloud before they are ready.

## New Words

- **desolation** — a state of bleak, empty devastation
- **canopy** — the upper layer of branches and leaves in a forest
- **confound** — to confuse or contradict someone's expectations
- **opportunistic** (of species) — quick to exploit newly available
  conditions
- **wasteland** — an area of land that is barren, ruined, or unused
- **catalogue** (verb, figurative) — to mentally classify or record
  something systematically
- **reconstitute** — to build or form something again after it has been
  broken down
- **mawkish** — excessively sentimental in a way that feels false or
  embarrassing

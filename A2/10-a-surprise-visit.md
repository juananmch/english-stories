# A Surprise Visit

Jennifer and Claire have been friends since university. Last year Claire moved
abroad for work, and since then they only talked through video calls.

In October Jennifer decided to do something about it. She booked a flight and
told nobody except Claire's roommate, who agreed to help.

She arrived on a Saturday morning after a short flight. In the taxi she felt
nervous and excited at the same time, like a child.

Claire's roommate opened the door quietly.

"She is in the kitchen," she whispered. "She has no idea."

Jennifer walked softly down the hall. Claire was making coffee and listening to
the radio, completely unaware.

"Excuse me," Jennifer said in a low voice. "Do you have any sugar?"

Claire turned around. For two full seconds she said nothing at all. Then she
dropped the spoon.

"No. No! What are you doing here?"

She ran across the kitchen and hugged Jennifer so hard that they almost fell.

"I missed you," Jennifer said. "Video calls are terrible."

They spent the whole day walking. Claire showed her the market, the river, the
small café where she goes every morning, and the ugly building where she works.

In the evening they cooked dinner in the apartment, exactly like they used to
do at university. They talked for hours about work, about Claire's new city,
about the friends they both miss.

"I still cannot believe you flew here without telling me," Claire said.

"That is the point of a surprise."

Before Jennifer left on Monday, they promised to visit each other twice a year.

"Distance does not change anything," Claire said at the airport. "But next
time, please, warn me before you appear in my kitchen. I dropped my best spoon."

Back home, Jennifer told Patricia everything.

"Was it worth it, for two days?" Patricia asked.

Jennifer thought about the two seconds of silence in the kitchen.

"Completely," she said.

## New Words

- **abroad** — in or to another country
- **roommate** — a person you share a home with
- **whisper** — to speak very quietly
- **softly** — quietly and gently
- **unaware** — not knowing that something is happening
- **drop** — to let something fall
- **hug** — to put your arms around somebody to show you like them
- **distance** — how far it is between two places
- **warn** — to tell somebody about something before it happens
- **worth** — good enough for the time or money it costs

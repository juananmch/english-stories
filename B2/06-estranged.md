# Estranged

Nora hadn't spoken to her father in almost six years when the hospital called.
He had suffered a stroke, the nurse explained, and while he was stable, the
next few days would be significant. Nora sat with the phone in her hand for a
long time after the call ended, unsure what, exactly, she was supposed to feel.

Their estrangement didn't stem from one dramatic event, which somehow made
it harder to resolve. It had accumulated slowly, through years of
disappointments, unmet expectations, and a stubbornness on both sides that
neither had ever been willing to name out loud. Her father had missed
significant moments in her life, citing work, distance, or simple forgetfulness.
Eventually, she had stopped expecting him to show up, and stopping expecting
it, she had realized, was its own kind of grief.

Her husband, Henry, drove her to the hospital, saying little, sensing she
needed space rather than advice. In the car, she found herself rehearsing
conversations she had imagined having for years — some furious, some
forgiving, none of them ever quite finished.

Her father looked smaller than she remembered, diminished by the hospital bed
and the equipment surrounding him. For a moment, neither of them spoke.

"I wasn't sure you'd come," he said finally, his voice unsteady.

"I wasn't sure either," she admitted.

They didn't resolve six years of distance in that single visit; that would
have been unrealistic, and she didn't pretend otherwise. But something began,
tentatively, to shift. He apologized, haltingly, for things she hadn't
expected him to acknowledge at all. She found herself, somewhat to her own
surprise, less interested in trying to assign blame than she had anticipated.

"If I'd had more time," he said, "I think I would have tried harder, sooner.
I kept assuming there would be time later."

"There's still some now," she said, though she wasn't entirely certain she
believed it.

Over the following weeks, as he slowly recovered, they spoke more than they
had in years — carefully, imperfectly, without any illusion that the past six
years could simply be erased. Driving home one evening, Nora found herself
telling Henry something she hadn't fully admitted to herself until that
moment.

"I wish I hadn't waited for something like this to happen," she said. "But I
don't wish I hadn't gone."

Henry didn't say much in response, mostly because he sensed she wasn't
looking for advice. He reached over and took her hand instead, and they drove
the rest of the way in a silence that felt, for the first time in a long
while, comfortable rather than heavy. Later that night, she called her father
just to say goodnight, something she hadn't done since she was a teenager,
and something she suspected, from the pause before he answered, he hadn't
expected either.

## New Words

- **estrangement** — a state in which people who were once close no longer
  speak or have a relationship
- **stem from** — to be caused by or originate from
- **stubbornness** — refusal to change one's mind or behavior
- **unsteady** — shaking or uncertain, not firm
- **tentatively** — cautiously, without full confidence
- **haltingly** — hesitantly, with pauses, as if uncertain
- **illusion** — a false belief or impression
- **assign blame** — to decide who is responsible for something bad

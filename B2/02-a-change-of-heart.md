# A Change of Heart

Nora had never been particularly political, which made it stranger, even to
her, how strongly she had opposed the new development plan when it was first
proposed. A large housing complex was to be built on the field behind her
street, and she had spent two months campaigning against it, convinced it
would ruin the character of the neighborhood.

Having lived on that street for fifteen years, she felt entitled to an
opinion, and she wasn't shy about sharing it. She organized a petition,
attended every council meeting, and argued, sometimes quite forcefully, with
anyone who suggested the development might actually be needed.

Her neighbor Felix, who worked for a housing charity, approached the issue
differently. Rather than arguing with her directly, he invited her to visit
the charity's shelter one afternoon, ostensibly just to see where he worked.

"I'm not trying to change your mind," he told her, though they both knew that
wasn't entirely true.

What she saw that afternoon unsettled her more than she expected. Families,
some with young children, were sleeping in a converted gymnasium, waiting for
housing that, as things stood, might not exist for years. One woman, having
been on a waiting list for over a year, told Nora she had almost given up
hope of ever having her own kitchen again.

Driving home, Nora found herself unable to shake the conversation. If she had
never visited the shelter, she told herself, she would have continued
believing her position was simply about preserving a view she liked. Now that
belief felt considerably harder to defend.

She didn't change her mind overnight. For a week, she avoided Felix
entirely, uncomfortable with how thoroughly her certainty had been shaken.
Eventually, though, she called him.

"I still don't love the design," she admitted. "But I can't keep arguing
against it the way I was. Not after that."

"That's more than I expected," Felix said.

"I wish I'd thought about it properly from the start," Nora said, "instead of
assuming I already understood the whole picture."

At the next council meeting, she didn't speak against the development. She
didn't speak in favor of it either, not yet — but for the first time in
months, she sat quietly and actually listened to what the other side was
saying, which, she realized, was something she probably should have done from
the beginning.

A few weeks later, she found herself defending the development to a neighbor
who had adopted the same position she once held. She didn't argue as
forcefully as she might have imagined a year earlier; instead, she found
herself repeating almost exactly what Felix had once said to her, careful not
to sound as though she was simply trying to change his mind. He listened
politely, unconvinced, and she recognized something uncomfortably familiar in
his expression — the same certainty she had once worn herself, before an
afternoon in a converted gymnasium had quietly taken it apart.

## New Words

- **entitled** — believing you have the right to something
- **unsettle** — to make someone feel uncertain or uneasy
- **converted** — changed in purpose or form from what it originally was
- **preserve** — to keep something as it is, without allowing change or damage
- **considerably** — by a large amount
- **thoroughly** — completely and carefully
